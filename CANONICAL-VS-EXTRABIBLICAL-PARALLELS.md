## 1. KINGDOM SAYINGS

### Kingdom Within / Among You

| Source | Text | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Luke 17:20-21** | "The kingdom of God is not coming with signs to be observed, nor will they say, 'Look, here it is!' or 'There!' for behold, the kingdom of God is in the midst of you [or within you]." | Greek *entos hymon* = "within you" or "among you" |
| **Gospel of Thomas 3** | "If your leaders say to you, 'Look, the kingdom is in the sky,' then the birds of the sky will precede you. If they say to you, 'It is in the sea,' then the fish will precede you. Rather, the kingdom is inside you and it is outside you." | Expands on Luke, adds "inside AND outside" |
| **Gospel of Thomas 113** | "His disciples said: When will the kingdom come? Jesus said: It will not come by waiting for it. They will not say: Look, here it is, or Look, there it is. Rather, the kingdom of the Father is spread out upon the earth, and people do not see it." | Very similar to Luke, adds "spread out upon earth" |
| **Gospel of Mary 8:14-15** | "Beware that no one lead you astray saying, 'Lo, here!' or 'Lo, there!' For the Son of Man is within you." | Similar to Luke, changes "kingdom" to "Son of Man" |

**Analysis:**
- **Core saying likely authentic:** "Not here or there, but within/among you"
- **Luke:** Ambiguous Greek allows "within" or "among"
- **Thomas 3:** Gnostic elaboration - kingdom internal AND external
- **Thomas 113:** Present, realized eschatology (not future)
- **Mary:** Internalizes further - Son of Man within

**Theological Shift:** Canonical = kingdom present in Jesus's ministry → Thomas = kingdom as self-knowledge

### Mustard Seed Parable

| Source | Text | Context |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Mark 4:30-32** | "With what can we compare the kingdom of God? It is like a grain of mustard seed, which, when sown on the ground, is the smallest of all the seeds on earth, yet when it is sown it grows up and becomes larger than all the garden plants and puts out large branches, so that the birds of the air can make nests in its shade." | Public teaching from boat |
| **Matthew 13:31-32** | "The kingdom of heaven is like a grain of mustard seed that a man took and sowed in his field. It is the smallest of all seeds, but when it has grown it is larger than all the garden plants and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches." | Parable discourse, similar to Mark |
| **Luke 13:18-19** | "What is the kingdom of God like? It is like a grain of mustard seed that a man took and sowed in his garden, and it grew and became a tree, and the birds of the air made nests in its branches." | Shorter, during journey to Jerusalem |
| **Gospel of Thomas 20** | "The disciples said to Jesus: Tell us what the kingdom of heaven is like. He said to them: It is like a mustard seed. It is the smallest of all seeds, but when it falls on cultivated ground, it produces a large branch and becomes shelter for the birds of the sky." | Q-like simplicity, "cultivated ground" detail |

**Analysis:**
- **All four agree:** Small seed → large plant → birds nest
- **Mark:** "Smallest of all seeds" (hyperbol, not botanically accurate)
- **Matthew:** Adds "field" detail, becomes "tree"
- **Luke:** Shortest version, "garden"
- **Thomas:** "Cultivated ground" - requires preparation (gnostic: prepared soul?)

**Growth Pattern:**
- Mark (earliest) → Matthew/Luke elaborate → Thomas simplifies OR Thomas preserves Q, Synoptics elaborate

**Key Difference:** Canonical emphasizes surprising growth of kingdom; Thomas may hint at need for "cultivated" (enlightened) ground

## 2. WISDOM & SEEKING

### Seek and Find

| Source | Text | Theme |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Matthew 7:7-8** | "Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened." | Prayer, persistence |
| **Luke 11:9-10** | "And I tell you, ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened." | Nearly identical to Matthew (Q source) |
| **Gospel of Thomas 2** | "Let him who seeks not cease seeking until he finds, and when he finds, he will be troubled, and when he has been troubled, he will marvel and he will reign over the All." | Seeking leads to troubling revelation |
| **Gospel of Thomas 92** | "Jesus said: Seek and you will find. But those things you asked me about in past times, which I did not tell you then, now I want to tell them, but you do not inquire about them." | Disciples aren't asking the right questions |
| **Gospel of Thomas 94** | "He who seeks will find; to him who knocks it will be opened." | Shortened form, similar to canonical |

**Analysis:**
- **Canonical:** Straightforward promise - seek/ask/knock → receive/find/opened
- **Thomas 2:** Adds stages: seek → find → troubled → marvel → reign
- **Thomas 92:** Ironic twist - offers to tell, but they don't ask
- **Thomas 94:** Closest to canonical

**Theological Shift:** 
- Canonical = God responds to petitions
- Thomas = Seeking gnosis leads through discomfort to enlightenment

### Hidden Revealed

| Source | Text | Application |
|--------|------|-------------|
| **Mark 4:22** | "For nothing is hidden except to be made manifest; nor is anything secret except to come to light." | Context: Parable interpretation for disciples |
| **Matthew 10:26** | "Nothing is covered that will not be revealed, or hidden that will not be known." | Context: Don't fear persecutors |
| **Luke 8:17** | "For nothing is hidden that will not be made manifest, nor is anything secret that will not be known and come to light." | Context: Parable of lamp |
| **Luke 12:2** | "Nothing is covered up that will not be revealed, or hidden that will not be known." | Context: Beware hypocrisy of Pharisees |
| **Gospel of Thomas 5** | "Know what is before your face, and what is hidden from you will be disclosed to you. For there is nothing hidden that will not be revealed." | Context: Seeking truth |
| **Gospel of Thomas 6** | "His disciples asked him: Do you want us to fast? How should we pray? Should we give alms? What diet should we observe? Jesus said: ... There is nothing hidden that will not be revealed, and there is nothing covered that will remain undisclosed." | Context: Religious practices questioned |

**Analysis:**
- **Core saying very stable** across all sources
- **Canonical contexts vary:** Disciples understanding parables (Mark), fearless witness (Matthew), hypocrisy exposed (Luke)
- **Thomas context:** Hidden spiritual knowledge will be revealed to seekers

**Consistency:** One of most stable sayings - appears 6 times with minimal variation

## 3. PARABLES - SHARED STORIES

### Parable of the Sower / Soils

| Source | Parable Text | Interpretation Given? |
|--------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Mark 4:3-9** | Sower went out to sow. Seeds fell on path (birds), rocky ground (no root), thorns (choked), good soil (30/60/100 fold). | Yes - Mark 4:13-20 (detailed) |
| **Matthew 13:3-9** | Nearly identical to Mark | Yes - Matt 13:18-23 (similar to Mark) |
| **Luke 8:5-8** | Similar to Mark/Matthew, simplified | Yes - Luke 8:11-15 (similar) |
| **Gospel of Thomas 9** | "Behold, the sower went out, filled his hand, threw. Some fell upon the road; birds came and gathered them. Others fell on rock and did not take root in earth nor produce ears upward to heaven. And others fell on thorns; they choked the seed and the worm ate them. And others fell upon the good earth; and it brought forth a good crop, yielding sixty per measure and one hundred twenty per measure." | NO interpretation given |

**Key Differences:**

| Element | Canonical | Thomas |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Rocky ground | "No root, withered" | "Did not take root nor produce ears upward to heaven" |
| Thorns | "Choked the plants" | "Choked and worm ate them" |
| Good soil yields | 30, 60, 100 fold | 60, 120 per measure |
| Interpretation | Given to disciples | NOT given - readers must understand |

**Analysis:**
- **Core story identical:** Four soils, different outcomes
- **Thomas unique details:** "Filled his hand" (human limitation?), "worm" (corruption), "upward to heaven" (spiritual aspiration)
- **Thomas yields:** 60, 120 (doubles) vs. canonical 30, 60, 100 (irregular)
- **Biggest difference:** Canonical explains parable; Thomas expects readers to figure it out (gnostic self-knowledge)

### Parable of Wicked Tenants

| Source | Key Elements | Ending |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Mark 12:1-11** | Owner plants vineyard → Sends servants (beaten/killed) → Sends son ("They will respect my son") → Tenants kill son → Owner destroys tenants, gives vineyard to others | Cornerstone quote (Psalm 118:22-23) |
| **Matthew 21:33-44** | Similar to Mark, more details | Adds: "Kingdom taken from you, given to people producing fruits" + cornerstone crushes |
| **Luke 20:9-18** | Similar to Mark/Matthew | Adds: People respond "Surely not!" to judgment |
| **Gospel of Thomas 65-66** | Owner sends servant (beaten) → Sends another servant (they beat him also) → Sends his son, thinking "Perhaps they will respect my son" → Tenants seized and killed him. Jesus said: "Show me the stone which the builders rejected; that is the cornerstone." | **NO judgment/destruction of tenants!** Just moves to cornerstone saying |

**Striking Difference:**

| Canonical | Thomas |
|-----------|--------|
| Climax: Owner destroys wicked tenants | NO destruction mentioned |
| Vineyard given to others | NOT mentioned |
| Clear judgment/punishment theme | Judgment absent - goes straight to cornerstone |
| Allegorical: Israel rejected → Church | Less clear allegory |

**Analysis:**
- **Thomas softens judgment:** Removes vineyard transfer and tenant destruction
- **Theological implication:** Canonical = rejection brings consequences; Thomas = focuses on builders/cornerstone without punishment
- **Possible reasons:** Thomas community didn't see themselves replacing Israel, or emphasized spiritual over judgment themes

### Parable of Lost Sheep

| Source | Context | Details | Conclusion |
|--------|---------|---------|------------|
| **Matthew 18:12-14** | Teaching on humility/children | Man has 100 sheep, one wanders, leaves 99 on mountains, goes after lost one. "If he finds it, truly I say he rejoices over it more than over 99 that never went astray." | "It is not will of my Father that one of these little ones should perish." |
| **Luke 15:4-7** | Response to Pharisees criticizing Jesus for eating with sinners | Man has 100 sheep, loses one, leaves 99 in open country, goes after lost one. Finds it, lays on shoulders rejoicing, calls friends: "Rejoice with me!" | "More joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over 99 righteous persons who need no repentance." |
| **Gospel of Thomas 107** | No context given | "The kingdom is like a shepherd who had 100 sheep. One of them, the largest, went astray. He left the 99 and sought the one until he found it. After he had exerted himself, he said to the sheep: I love you more than the 99." | **NO religious/theological conclusion** |

**Key Differences:**

| Element | Matthew | Luke | Thomas |
|---------|---------|------|--------|
| Lost sheep is | One that wanders | One that is lost | **The largest/best** |
| Shepherd's action | Goes after it | Goes, finds, carries on shoulders | Seeks until found |
| Emotional climax | Rejoices over it more | Calls friends to celebrate | **"I love you more than 99"** |
| Theological point | Father doesn't want little ones to perish | Joy in heaven over repentant sinner | **NONE - just love** |
| Audience | Disciples (internal) | Pharisees (external critics) | Unknown |

**Analysis:**
- **Matthew:** Pastoral care, protecting vulnerable
- **Luke:** Joy over repentance, justifying Jesus's association with sinners  
- **Thomas:** Pure love, no religious framework. Lost sheep is "largest" (most valuable? most enlightened who strayed?)
- **Thomas removes:** All theological interpretation - just shepherd's love for special sheep

**Possible meanings:**
- Canonical: God seeks lost sinners
- Thomas: Divine pursues the spiritually exceptional who stray? Or simply: love transcends numbers?

## 4. ETHICS & BEHAVIOR

### Love of Enemies

| Source | Command | Elaboration |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Matthew 5:43-48** | "You have heard that it was said, 'You shall love your neighbor and hate your enemy.' But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be sons of your Father who is in heaven." | Elaboration: God makes sun rise on evil and good, rain on just and unjust. Be perfect as Father is perfect. |
| **Luke 6:27-36** | "But I say to you who hear, Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you, bless those who curse you, pray for those who abuse you." | Elaboration: Turn other cheek, give tunic, lend expecting nothing, be merciful as Father is merciful. Golden Rule follows. |
| **Gospel of Thomas 25** | "Love your brother as your soul; watch over him like the apple of your eye." | **NOT enemies - just "brother"!** |

**Striking Absence:** Thomas has NO "love enemies" saying, changes to "love your brother"

**Analysis:**
- **Canonical (both Matthew & Luke):** Radical love extending to enemies - core Jesus teaching
- **Thomas:** Softens to "brother" - fellow Gnostics? Or preserves earlier "love neighbor" stage?
- **Significance:** One of most radical canonical sayings absent from Thomas

### Two Masters

| Source | Text | Application |
|--------|------|-------------|
| **Matthew 6:24** | "No one can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money." | Context: Don't be anxious, seek God's kingdom first |
| **Luke 16:13** | "No servant can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money." | Context: Parable of dishonest manager, Pharisees love money |
| **Gospel of Thomas 47a** | "It is impossible for a person to mount two horses or stretch two bows, and it is impossible for a servant to serve two masters, or he will honor the one and despise the other." | **Adds concrete examples: two horses, two bows** |

**Analysis:**
- **Core saying identical** across all three
- **Thomas addition:** "Mount two horses, stretch two bows" - may be original, canonical simplified?
- **Context:** Canonical applies to God vs. money; Thomas context unknown
- **Wisdom:** Universal truth about divided loyalty

### Beatitudes - Blessed Are the Poor

| Source | Form | Who Is Blessed? |
|--------|------|----------------|
| **Matthew 5:3-12** | "Blessed are the **poor in spirit**, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven." [+ 8 more beatitudes] | Poor in spirit, mourning, meek, hungry for righteousness, merciful, pure in heart, peacemakers, persecuted |
| **Luke 6:20-23** | "Blessed are you who are **poor**, for yours is the kingdom of God." [+ 3 more beatitudes + 4 woes] | You poor, you hungry now, you weeping now, you when persecuted |
| **Gospel of Thomas 54** | "Blessed are the **poor**, for yours is the kingdom of heaven." | Just the poor - no elaboration |

**Key Difference:**

| Matthew | Luke | Thomas |
|---------|------|--------|
| "Poor in spirit" (spiritual poverty) | "Poor" (literal poverty) | "Poor" (literal) |
| 9 beatitudes | 4 beatitudes + 4 woes | 1 beatitude (poor only) |
| Third person ("Blessed are...") | Second person ("Blessed are YOU...") | Second person (following Luke) |
| Spiritualized | Material/literal | Literal |

**Analysis:**
- **Thomas = Luke version** (literal poor), not Matthew's spiritual poverty
- **Scholarly consensus:** Luke/Thomas preserve earlier form; Matthew spiritualized it
- **Thomas simplest:** Just one beatitude, may be closest to original
- **Evolution:** "Poor" → Luke's 4 beatitudes + woes → Matthew's 9 spiritualized beatitudes

## 5. IDENTITY & AUTHORITY

### Prophet Without Honor

| Source | Text | Setting |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Mark 6:4** | "A prophet is not without honor, except in his hometown and among his relatives and in his own household." | Rejected in Nazareth, couldn't do many miracles there |
| **Matthew 13:57** | "A prophet is not without honor except in his hometown and in his own household." | Similar to Mark, Nazareth rejection |
| **Luke 4:24** | "Truly, I say to you, no prophet is acceptable in his hometown." | Nazareth synagogue, nearly thrown off cliff |
| **John 4:44** | "A prophet has no honor in his own hometown." | Brief reference, traveling through Galilee |
| **Gospel of Thomas 31** | "No prophet is acceptable in his own village; a doctor does not heal those who know him." | **Adds "doctor" parallel** |
| **Papyrus Oxyrhynchus 1** (Greek Thomas) | "A prophet is not acceptable in his hometown, nor does a physician work cures on those who know him." | Same as Thomas 31 |

**Analysis:**
- **Saying appears 6 times** - one of most attested
- **Thomas unique addition:** "Doctor does not heal those who know him"
- **Possible interpretations:** 
  - Thomas preserves fuller original saying?
  - Doctor parallel added later to reinforce prophet saying?
- **Consistency:** Core "prophet without honor at home" is bedrock saying

### Light of the World

| Source | Text | Context |
|--------|------|---------|
| **John 8:12** | "I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life." | Temple treasury, Feast of Tabernacles |
| **John 9:5** | "As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world." | Before healing blind man |
| **Matthew 5:14** | "You are the light of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hidden." | Sermon on Mount - **disciples are light** |
| **Gospel of Thomas 77** | "I am the light that is over all. I am the All. The All came forth out of me, and the All attained unto me." | **Cosmic claim - Jesus = All** |

**Major Difference:**

| John | Matthew | Thomas |
|------|---------|--------|
| **"I am the light"** | **"You [disciples] are the light"** | **"I am the light over All"** |
| Metaphor: spiritual illumination | Metaphor: disciples' witness | Ontological: Jesus IS the universe |
| Dualism: light vs. darkness | Ethical: be visible witness | Pantheism/Gnosticism: Jesus = cosmos |

**Analysis:**
- **John:** Jesus is light (Johannine "I AM" saying)
- **Matthew:** Disciples are light (ethical imperative)
- **Thomas:** Jesus is cosmic light over all existence (Gnostic)
- **These may not be variants** of same saying, but independent uses of "light" metaphor

## 6. ESCHATOLOGY & FUTURE

### Day and Hour Unknown

| Source | Text | Context |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Mark 13:32** | "But concerning that day or that hour, no one knows, not even the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father." | Olivet Discourse, signs before the end |
| **Matthew 24:36** | "But concerning that day and hour no one knows, not even the angels of heaven, nor the Son, but the Father only." | Olivet Discourse, nearly identical to Mark |
| **Gospel of Thomas 113** | "His disciples said to him: When will the kingdom come? Jesus said: It will not come by waiting for it." | **Denies future coming entirely** |

**Radical Difference:**

| Canonical | Thomas |
|-----------|--------|
| Day/hour WILL come, but unknown | Kingdom does NOT come in future |
| Future eschatology (apocalyptic) | Present eschatology (realized) |
| "No one knows when" | "Not by waiting for it" |
| Temporal question gets temporal answer | Temporal question rejected |

**Theological Chasm:**
- **Canonical:** Kingdom is future event, date unknown
- **Thomas:** Kingdom is present reality, not future arrival
- **Cannot reconcile:** These represent fundamentally different eschatologies

## 7. WOES & WARNINGS

### Woes to Scribes and Pharisees

| Source | Form | Number of Woes |
|--------|------|----------------|
| **Matthew 23:13-36** | "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you..." | **7 woes** with detailed elaborations |
| **Luke 11:42-52** | "Woe to you Pharisees! For you..." | **6 woes** (3 to Pharisees, 3 to lawyers) |
| **Gospel of Thomas 39** | "The Pharisees and the scribes have taken the keys of knowledge and have hidden them. They did not go in, and those who wanted to go in they did not allow." | **No "woe" - just statement of fact** |
| **Gospel of Thomas 102** | "Woe to the Pharisees! For they are like a dog sleeping in the feeding trough of cattle, for it neither eats nor lets the cattle eat." | **1 woe only**, with colorful metaphor |

**Analysis:**

| Matthew | Luke | Thomas 39 | Thomas 102 |
|---------|------|-----------|------------|
| 7 woes, harsh polemic | 6 woes, somewhat shorter | Statement without "woe" | 1 woe, different metaphor |
| "Hypocrites" repeated | "Lawyers" addressed | Hidden keys of knowledge | Dog in feeding trough |
| Detailed accusations | Concise accusations | Brief | Brief |
| Public confrontation | Dinner at Pharisee's house | Unknown context | Unknown context |

**Metaphors:**
- **Matthew:** Whitewashed tombs, blind guides, snakes, brood of vipers
- **Thomas 102:** Dog sleeping in feeding trough (doesn't eat, won't let cattle eat)
- **Thomas 39:** Keys of knowledge hidden (similar to Luke 11:52)

**Tone:** Canonical is harsher, more confrontational; Thomas preserves criticism but less vehement

## 8. UNIQUE EXTRA-BIBLICAL SAYINGS

### Sayings with NO Canonical Parallel

#### Gospel of Thomas 77 - Split Wood, Raise Stone
> "Split wood—I am there. Raise up the stone, and you shall find me there."

**Scholarly Opinion:** 
- May preserve authentic wisdom saying
- Divine immanence in ordinary work
- Cited by Origen as authentic Jesus saying (from unknown source)
- **Authenticity: MODERATE** - Could be genuine

---

#### Gospel of Thomas 82 - Near Fire
> "Whoever is near me is near the fire, and whoever is far from me is far from the kingdom."

**Scholarly Opinion:**
- Origen cites this as Jesus saying
- Fits Jesus's eschatological urgency
- Fire = testing/purification/judgment?
- **Authenticity: MODERATE** - Multiple attestation (Thomas + Origen)

---

#### Agrapha - "Be Approved Money-Changers"
> "Be tested/approved money-changers [bankers]"

**Sources:** 
- Clement of Alexandria (*Stromata*)
- Origen (*Commentary on John*)
- Pseudo-Clementine Homilies

**Meaning:** Test/discern truth from falsehood, like bankers test coins for authenticity

**Authenticity: MODERATE** - Multiple early sources, fits Jesus's teaching style

---

#### Acts 20:35 - "More Blessed to Give"
> "It is more blessed to give than to receive."

**Source:** Paul quotes Jesus in Acts

**Scholarly Opinion:**
- **Authenticity: HIGH** - Paul claims it's from Jesus
- Not in any gospel, but preserved in Acts
- Fits Jesus's teaching on generosity and service
- May be from oral tradition Paul received

## PATTERNS & OBSERVATIONS

### Sayings Most Stable Across Sources:
1. **Prophet without honor** (6 sources)
2. **Hidden will be revealed** (6 sources)
3. **Seek and find** (5 sources)
4. **Two masters** (3 sources, identical)
5. **Kingdom within/among** (4 sources, similar)

### Greatest Variations:
1. **Beatitudes** - Matthew (9 spiritual), Luke (4 material + woes), Thomas (1 literal)
2. **Parable of Lost Sheep** - Canonical (theological), Thomas (just love)
3. **Eschatology** - Canonical (future kingdom), Thomas (present kingdom)
4. **Woes** - Matthew (7 detailed), Luke (6 shorter), Thomas (1-2 brief)

### Thomas Additions (No Canonical Parallel):
- Split wood/raise stone (saying 77)
- Near fire/far from kingdom (saying 82)
- Making male and female one (saying 22, 114)
- Gnostic cosmology (multiple sayings)

### Canonical Additions (No Thomas Parallel):
- Love your enemies (missing in Thomas!)
- Lord's Prayer (absent from Thomas)
- Most miracle stories
- Passion predictions
- Most eschatological/apocalyptic material

## THEOLOGICAL TRAJECTORIES

### From Thomas → Canonical (or vice versa?)

**If Thomas preserves earlier tradition:**
- Kingdom sayings were originally about present, internal reality
- Later apocalypticized in canonical gospels
- Shorter, wisdom-focused sayings expanded

**If Canonical preserves earlier tradition:**
- Thomas removed apocalyptic/judgment themes
- Spiritualized/internalized kingdom teaching
- Simplified complex parables

**Scholarly Consensus Leans:** Mix of both
- Some Thomas sayings may be earlier (kingdom within, simple beatitude)
- Some are later Gnostic elaborations (cosmology, gender sayings)
- Most canonical material is earlier than Thomas compilation
