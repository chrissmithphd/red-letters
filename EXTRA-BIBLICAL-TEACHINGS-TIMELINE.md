# Extra-Biblical Teachings Timeline

## Important Context

**Dating Challenge:** Unlike canonical gospels, extra-biblical sources cannot be dated to Jesus's ministry (27-30 CE). The dates below refer to **when the texts were composed**, not when Jesus allegedly spoke these words.

**Authenticity Note:** Scholars debate whether any extra-biblical sayings preserve authentic oral tradition. Most are considered 2nd-3rd century theological compositions, though some (particularly in Gospel of Thomas) may contain earlier material.

---

## TIMELINE BY SOURCE COMPOSITION

### 1st CENTURY CE (Potential Early Material)

#### JOSEPHUS - Testimonium Flavianum (93-94 CE)
**Source:** *Antiquities of the Jews*, Book 18, Chapter 3, Section 3  
**Language:** Greek (possibly from Aramaic sources)  
**Author:** Flavius Josephus (Jewish historian, not a Christian)

**The Text (Disputed Version):**
> "About this time there lived Jesus, a wise man, if indeed one ought to call him a man. For he was one who performed surprising deeds and was a teacher of such people as accept the truth gladly. He won over many Jews and many of the Greeks. He was the Christ. And when, upon the accusation of the principal men among us, Pilate had condemned him to a cross, those who had first come to love him did not cease. He appeared to them spending a third day restored to life, for the prophets of God had foretold these things and a thousand other marvels about him. And the tribe of the Christians, so called after him, has still to this day not disappeared."

**Scholarly Consensus:**
- **Core authentic** - Josephus did mention Jesus
- **Christian interpolations** - "He was the Christ," "restored to life" likely added by Christian scribes
- **Reconstructed authentic core:** "About this time there lived Jesus, a wise man. He was a teacher... Pilate condemned him to a cross. The tribe of Christians... has not disappeared."

**Jesus Sayings:** None - only describes Jesus, no direct quotes

---

#### AGRAPHA - Unwritten Sayings (Late 1st-Early 2nd Century)
**Source:** Oral tradition preserved by early Church Fathers  
**Earliest Citations:** Papias (~110 CE), Clement of Alexandria (~200 CE)

**Notable Sayings:**

**1. From Papias (via Eusebius):**
> "The days will come in which vines shall grow, having each ten thousand branches, and in each branch ten thousand twigs, and in each twig ten thousand shoots, and in every one of the shoots ten thousand clusters, and on every one of the clusters ten thousand grapes, and every grape when pressed will give five-and-twenty metretes of wine."

**Context:** Eschatological prophecy about abundance in the Kingdom  
**Authenticity:** LOW - likely later embellishment

**2. "Be approved money-changers"** (Clement of Alexandria, *Stromata* 1.28.177)
> "Γίνεσθε τραπεζῖται δόκιμοι" (*Ginesthe trapezitai dokimoi*)
> "Be tested/approved money-changers [bankers]"

**Context:** Discern truth from falsehood, test everything  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - Could be authentic, appears in several sources  
**Parallel:** 1 Thessalonians 5:21 "Test everything, hold fast what is good"

**3. From Codex Bezae (Acts 20:35 addition):**
> "It is more blessed to give than to receive"

**Context:** Paul quotes this as a saying of Jesus  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Multiple attestation, fits Jesus's teaching style  
**Note:** Not in any gospel, but preserved in Acts

### EARLY 2nd CENTURY (80-150 CE)

#### GOSPEL OF THOMAS (80-140 CE, Greek fragments ~200 CE)
**Manuscript:** Nag Hammadi Codex II (~350 CE Coptic), P.Oxy 1, 654, 655 (Greek)  
**Format:** 114 sayings, no narrative  
**Theological Perspective:** Proto-Gnostic/Wisdom tradition

**Organization:** By saying number with canonical parallels noted

**SAYING 1:**
> "Whoever finds the interpretation of these sayings will not taste death."

**Canonical Parallel:** None (similar to John 8:51 "whoever keeps my word will never see death")  
**Authenticity:** LOW - Gnostic framing

**SAYING 2:**
> "Let him who seeks not cease seeking until he finds, and when he finds, he will be troubled, and when he has been troubled, he will marvel and he will reign over the All."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 7:7-8 "Seek and you will find"  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - May be variant of Q source saying  
**Theme:** Seeking leads to troubling truth, then enlightenment

**SAYING 3:**
> "If your leaders say to you, 'Look, the kingdom is in the sky,' then the birds of the sky will precede you. If they say to you, 'It is in the sea,' then the fish will precede you. Rather, the kingdom is inside you and it is outside you."

**Canonical Parallel:** Luke 17:21 "The kingdom of God is within you"  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - Could preserve early tradition about internal kingdom  
**Note:** Continues with "When you know yourselves, then you will be known"

**SAYING 5:**
> "Know what is before your face, and what is hidden from you will be disclosed to you. For there is nothing hidden that will not be revealed."

**Canonical Parallel:** Mark 4:22, Luke 8:17 "Nothing is hidden except to be made manifest"  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Very close to canonical version  
**Note:** May share common source with Mark

**SAYING 9 - Parable of the Sower (parallels Mark 4:3-9)**
[See Thematic Comparison Tables below]

**SAYING 13 - Jesus's Identity:**
> "Jesus said to his disciples: Make a comparison and tell me whom I am like. Simon Peter said to him: You are like a righteous angel. Matthew said to him: You are like a wise philosopher. Thomas said to him: Master, my mouth is wholly incapable of saying whom you are like. Jesus said: I am not your master, because you have drunk, you have become intoxicated from the bubbling spring which I have measured out."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 16:13-16 (Caesarea Philippi confession), but completely different  
**Authenticity:** LOW - Gnostic reworking of canonical scene  
**Note:** Thomas elevated over Peter (typical of Thomas community)

**SAYING 17:**
> "I will give you what no eye has seen, what no ear has heard, what no hand has touched, what has not arisen in the human heart."

**Canonical Parallel:** 1 Corinthians 2:9 (Paul quoting Isaiah 64:4 or unknown source)  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - May preserve pre-Pauline saying  
**Note:** Paul attributes it to scripture, Thomas to Jesus

**SAYING 20 - Mustard Seed (parallels Mark 4:30-32)**
[See Thematic Comparison Tables]

**SAYING 22 - Infants Nursing:**
> "When you make the two into one, and when you make the inner like the outer and the outer like the inner, and the upper like the lower, and when you make male and female into a single one, so that the male will not be male nor the female be female... then you will enter the kingdom."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 18:3 "Unless you turn and become like children"  
**Authenticity:** LOW - Gnostic elaboration on canonical saying  
**Theme:** Unity of opposites, return to pre-fall androgyny

**SAYING 31:**
> "No prophet is acceptable in his own village; a doctor does not heal those who know him."

**Canonical Parallel:** Mark 6:4, Luke 4:24 "A prophet is not without honor except in his hometown"  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Very close to canonical, may share source  
**Note:** Adds "doctor" comparison

**SAYING 32:**
> "A city built on a high hill and fortified cannot fall nor can it be hidden."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 5:14 "A city set on a hill cannot be hidden"  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Nearly identical  
**Note:** Adds "fortified cannot fall" - may be original, canonical abbreviated?

**SAYING 33:**
> "What you hear in your ear, proclaim from your rooftops. For no one lights a lamp and puts it under a basket, nor in a hidden place, but sets it on a lampstand so that all who come and go may see its light."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 5:15, 10:27; Luke 8:16  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Combines multiple canonical sayings  
**Note:** May preserve pre-gospel collection

**SAYING 39:**
> "The Pharisees and the scribes have taken the keys of knowledge and have hidden them. They did not go in, and those who wanted to go in they did not allow."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 23:13 "Woe to you... you shut the kingdom of heaven against people"  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE to HIGH - Similar theme, different wording  
**Note:** Less harsh than canonical "woe" version

**SAYING 47:**
> "It is impossible for a person to mount two horses or stretch two bows, and it is impossible for a servant to serve two masters, or he will honor the one and despise the other."

**Canonical Parallel:** Matthew 6:24, Luke 16:13 "No one can serve two masters"  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Core saying likely authentic, Thomas adds examples  
**Note:** "Two horses/two bows" may be original imagery

**SAYING 54:**
> "Blessed are the poor, for yours is the kingdom of heaven."

**Canonical Parallel:** Luke 6:20 "Blessed are you who are poor, yours is kingdom of God"  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Nearly identical to Luke's version  
**Note:** Matthew spiritualizes ("poor in spirit"), Luke/Thomas preserve "poor"

**SAYING 55:**
> "Whoever does not hate father and mother cannot be my disciple, and whoever does not hate brothers and sisters and bear the cross as I do will not be worthy of me."

**Canonical Parallel:** Luke 14:26-27 "If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother..."  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Very close to Luke  
**Note:** "Bear cross as I do" - adds personal example

**SAYING 63 - Rich Fool Parable (parallels Luke 12:16-21)**
[See Thematic Comparison Tables]

**SAYING 65 - Wicked Tenants (parallels Mark 12:1-11)**
[See Thematic Comparison Tables]

**SAYING 77 - MOST CONTROVERSIAL:**
> "Jesus said: I am the light that is over all. I am the All. The All came forth out of me, and the All attained unto me. Split wood—I am there. Raise up the stone, and you shall find me there."

**Canonical Parallel:** Partial - John 8:12 "I am the light of the world"  
**Authenticity:** LOW for full saying, MODERATE for "split wood" line  
**Note:** "Split wood/raise stone" may preserve authentic wisdom saying about divine immanence  
**Scholarly Opinion:** Core idea (God present in ordinary work) could be early, cosmic claims are Gnostic

**SAYING 82:**
> "Whoever is near me is near the fire, and whoever is far from me is far from the kingdom."

**Canonical Parallel:** None in canonical gospels  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - Fits Jesus's style, found in Origen (*Homily on Jeremiah* 20.3)  
**Note:** Origen cites this as authentic Jesus saying from unknown source

**SAYING 100:**
> "Give to Caesar what is Caesar's, give to God what is God's, and give to me what is mine."

**Canonical Parallel:** Mark 12:17 "Render to Caesar... to God what is God's"  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - Canonical core + Thomas addition  
**Note:** "Give to me what is mine" - Gnostic addition claiming direct allegiance

**SAYING 107 - Lost Sheep (parallels Matthew 18:12-14)**
[See Thematic Comparison Tables]

**SAYING 113 - Kingdom Coming:**
> "His disciples said to him: When will the kingdom come? Jesus said: It will not come by waiting for it. They will not say: Look, here it is, or Look, there it is. Rather, the kingdom of the Father is spread out upon the earth, and people do not see it."

**Canonical Parallel:** Luke 17:20-21 "The kingdom of God is not coming with signs... it is in your midst"  
**Authenticity:** HIGH - Very similar to Luke, may share Q source  
**Theme:** Present, realized eschatology vs. future apocalyptic

**SAYING 114 - MOST CONTROVERSIAL (Gender):**
> "Simon Peter said to them: Let Mary go out from among us, because women are not worthy of the Life. Jesus said: See, I shall lead her, so that I will make her male, that she too may become a living spirit, resembling you males. For every woman who makes herself male will enter the Kingdom of Heaven."

**Canonical Parallel:** None  
**Authenticity:** LOW - Reflects 2nd century Gnostic gender theology  
**Note:** "Male" = spiritual perfection in Gnostic thought, not literal gender change  
**Context:** Debates about women's spiritual capacity in early Christianity

#### PAPYRUS EGERTON 2 - "Egerton Gospel" (~150 CE)
**Manuscript:** Egerton Papyrus 2 + Cologne fragments  
**Date:** ~150 CE (among earliest gospel fragments)  
**Content:** Four partial scenes, independent of canonical gospels

**Fragment 1 - Healing of Leper:**
> "And behold a leper came to him and said: Teacher Jesus, journeying with lepers and eating with them in the inn, I also became a leper. If therefore you will, I am clean. The Lord said to him: I will, be clean. And immediately the leprosy left him."

**Canonical Parallel:** Mark 1:40-42, Matthew 8:2-3, Luke 5:12-13  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - May preserve variant tradition  
**Unique Detail:** Explains how he became a leper (traveling with lepers)

**Fragment 2 - Question about Taxes:**
> "Teacher Jesus, we know that you have come from God, for what you do testifies beyond all the prophets. Tell us therefore: Is it lawful to pay to kings what pertains to their rule? Should we pay or not?"

**Canonical Parallel:** Mark 12:14-17 (Tribute to Caesar)  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE - Similar to canonical, different wording  
**Note:** Jesus's answer is fragmentary in papyrus

**Fragment 3 - Question about Scripture:**
> "You search the scriptures, in which you think you have life; they are the ones testifying about me. Do not think that I came to accuse you to my Father; there is one who accuses you, Moses, in whom you have hoped."

**Canonical Parallel:** John 5:39, 45-46  
**Authenticity:** MODERATE to HIGH - Very similar to John  
**Note:** May be from same tradition as John or influence John

### MID 2nd CENTURY (150-180 CE)

#### GOSPEL OF MARY (MAGDALENE) (~150-180 CE)
**Manuscript:** Papyrus Berolinensis 8502 (5th century), P.Rylands 463 (3rd century Greek)  
**Condition:** About 50% missing  
**Perspective:** Mary Magdalene's authority challenged by Peter

**Jesus to Disciples (before departure):**
> "Beware that no one lead you astray saying, 'Lo, here!' or 'Lo, there!' For the Son of Man is within you. Follow after him! Those who seek him will find him."

**Canonical Parallel:** Luke 17:21 "Kingdom of God is within you"  
**Authenticity:** LOW - 2nd century adaptation of canonical theme  
**Theme:** Internal vs. external salvation

**Peter to Mary:**
> "Sister, we know that the Savior loved you more than the rest of women. Tell us the words of the Savior which you remember—which you know but we do not, nor have we heard them."

**Jesus Sayings:** Not preserved - the vision Mary recounts is lost in missing pages

---

#### GOSPEL OF PHILIP (~180-250 CE)
**Manuscript:** Nag Hammadi Codex II  
**Format:** Excerpts and meditations, not continuous narrative  
**Perspective:** Valentinian Gnostic

**On Resurrection:**
> "Those who say, 'The Lord first died and then rose,' are mistaken. For he first rose and then died. If someone does not first attain the resurrection, will that person not die?"

**Canonical Parallel:** Contradicts canonical passion narrative  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Gnostic reinterpretation  
**Theme:** Spiritual resurrection before physical death

**On Mary Magdalene:**
> "The companion of the [Savior is] Mary Magdalene. [But Christ loved] her more than [all] the disciples, and used to kiss her [often] on her [mouth]. The rest of [the disciples were offended by it and expressed disapproval]. They said to him, 'Why do you love her more than all of us?'"

**Canonical Parallel:** None  
**Authenticity:** NONE - 2nd century Gnostic narrative  
**Note:** Brackets = damaged text; "mouth" is scholarly restoration

**On Bridal Chamber:**
> "The Lord did everything in a mystery: baptism, chrism, eucharist, redemption, and bridal chamber."

**Canonical Parallel:** None  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Valentinian sacramental theology  
**Context:** Introduces fifth sacrament not in canonical tradition

### LATE 2nd-3rd CENTURY (180-300 CE)

#### INFANCY GOSPEL OF THOMAS (~150-200 CE)
**Content:** Childhood stories of Jesus (ages 5-12)  
**Perspective:** Popular folklore, not Gnostic  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Legendary material

**Examples (showing why these are considered legendary):**

**Clay Birds Story (Age 5):**
> "Jesus made soft clay and fashioned from it twelve sparrows... Then Jesus clapped his hands and cried to the sparrows: 'Off with you!' And the sparrows took flight and went away chirping."

**Withering the Boy (Age 5):**
> "A boy ran and knocked against his shoulder. Jesus was angered and said to him: 'You shall not go further on your way.' And immediately the boy fell down and died."

**Cursing the Teacher:**
> "Woe to you, you unlearned elder! You try to teach me who know these things from the beginning."

**Purpose:** Demonstrates divine power even as child, but character is vindictive  
**Historical Value:** Shows popular imagination, not historical record

### 3rd-5th CENTURY SOURCES

#### TALMUD REFERENCES (200-500 CE)
**Source:** Babylonian Talmud (compiled 200-500 CE)  
**Language:** Aramaic/Hebrew  
**Perspective:** Jewish polemic against Christianity

**Sanhedrin 43a:**
> "On the eve of Passover they hanged Yeshu. And an announcer went out before him for forty days saying: 'He is going to be stoned because he practiced magic and enticed Israel to idolatry. Anyone who knows anything in his favor, let him come and declare concerning him.' But nothing was found in his favor, and they hanged him on the eve of Passover."

**Historical Value:** Confirms crucifixion timing (Passover eve), but hostile source  
**Jesus Sayings:** None - only describes accusations  
**Note:** "Magic" = miracles reframed negatively

**Sanhedrin 107b:**
> "Yeshu practiced magic and led Israel astray."

**Historical Value:** Acknowledges Jesus's influence  
**Jesus Sayings:** None

**Language:** Arabic (claims Syriac original)  
**Content:** Jesus's childhood, birth stories  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Late legendary material

### ISLAMIC SOURCES (7th Century CE)

#### QURAN (610-632 CE)
**Source:** Islamic revelation claimed by Muhammad  
**Language:** Classical Arabic  
**Content:** Isa (Jesus) mentioned in 25 surahs, 93 verses

**Key Quranic Statements about/by Jesus:**

**From the Cradle (Surah 19:30-33):**
> "He [baby Jesus] said: 'I am indeed a servant of Allah. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet. And He has made me blessed wherever I am and has enjoined upon me prayer and zakah as long as I remain alive. And [made me] dutiful to my mother, and He has not made me a wretched tyrant. And peace is on me the day I was born and the day I will die and the day I am raised alive.'"

**Authenticity:** NONE - Islamic theological claim  
**Context:** Baby Jesus speaks from cradle defending Mary's honor  
**Note:** Not in any Christian source, includes Islamic practices (zakah)

**Creating Birds (Surah 3:49):**
> "I design for you from clay [something] like the form of a bird, then I breathe into it and it becomes a bird by permission of Allah."

**Canonical Parallel:** Infancy Gospel of Thomas (clay sparrows)  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Legendary material  
**Note:** Quran may preserve Christian folk tradition

**Denying Divinity (Surah 5:116):**
> "And when Allah will say, 'O Jesus, Son of Mary, did you say to the people, "Take me and my mother as deities besides Allah?"' He will say, 'Exalted are You! It was not for me to say that to which I have no right.'"

**Canonical Parallel:** Contradicts Christian doctrine  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Islamic correction of Christian claims  
**Context:** Quran denies Trinity, Jesus portrayed as prophet only

**Second Coming (Surah 43:61):**
> "And indeed, Jesus will be [a sign for] knowledge of the Hour, so be not in doubt of it, and follow Me. This is a straight path."

**Authenticity:** NONE - Islamic eschatology  
**Note:** Islam teaches Jesus returns at end times, but as prophet not divine

**Table of Food (Surah 5:112-115):**
> "When the disciples said, 'O Jesus, Son of Mary, can your Lord send down to us a table from the heaven?' [Jesus] said, 'Fear Allah, if you should be believers.'... Said Jesus, 'O Allah, our Lord, send down to us a table from the heaven...'"

**Canonical Parallel:** Feeding miracles, but not requesting table from heaven  
**Authenticity:** NONE - Elaboration on gospel feeding stories  
**Context:** Shows Jesus as intercessor/prophet

## SUMMARY STATISTICS

### By Century:
- **1st Century CE:** Josephus (no sayings), Agrapha (3-4 possible sayings)
- **Early 2nd Century:** Gospel of Thomas (114 sayings, ~30-40 with moderate-high authenticity)
- **Mid 2nd Century:** Egerton Gospel (fragments), Gospel of Mary (minimal preserved)
- **Late 2nd-3rd Century:** Gospel of Philip (15-17 sayings, low authenticity), Infancy Thomas (legendary)
- **3rd-5th Century:** Talmud (hostile references, no sayings)
- **7th Century:** Quran (theological claims, not historical)

### Authenticity Assessment:
**HIGH potential (20-30 sayings):**
- Gospel of Thomas: Sayings 5, 31, 32, 33, 47, 54, 55, 113 (parallels canonical closely)
- Agrapha: "More blessed to give than receive" (Acts 20:35)
- Egerton Gospel: Fragments may preserve variant traditions

**MODERATE potential (30-40 sayings):**
- Gospel of Thomas: Sayings 2, 3, 17, 39, 77 (partial), 82, 100 (partial)
- Some sayings may preserve Q source or independent oral tradition

**LOW to NONE (100+ sayings):**
- Most Gnostic elaborations
- Infancy gospels
- Quranic material (theological, not historical)
- Gospel of Philip (Valentinian theology)

### Most Important Extra-Biblical Sources:
1. **Gospel of Thomas** - Potential pre-gospel oral tradition
2. **Agrapha** - Early Church Fathers preserving oral sayings
3. **Egerton Gospel** - Early independent gospel fragment
4. **Josephus** - Historical confirmation (no sayings)

### Least Reliable:
1. Infancy gospels (pure folklore)
2. Quranic material (7th century theology)
3. Talmud (hostile polemic)
4. Late Gnostic texts (theological compositions)

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

### What Extra-Biblical Sources Add:

**Potential Authentic Material:**
- ~10-15 sayings may preserve independent 1st century tradition
- Variants of canonical sayings may show how they circulated
- "Split wood/raise stone" may preserve authentic wisdom saying
- "Be approved money-changers" has multiple attestation

**Historical Context:**
- Josephus confirms Jesus existed, was executed under Pilate
- Shows diversity of early Christianity (Gnostic, Jewish-Christian)
- Demonstrates how Jesus traditions evolved and were adapted

**What They DON'T Add:**
- No new biographical details
- No reliable chronology
- No independent confirmation of miracles
- Mostly theological elaborations, not historical memories

### Scholarly Consensus:
- Gospel of Thomas is most important extra-biblical source
- A few sayings may be as early as canonical gospels
- Most extra-biblical material is 2nd-3rd century composition
- No extra-biblical source is as reliable as canonical gospels for Jesus's teaching
