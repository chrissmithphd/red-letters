## PART 1: NAG HAMMADI LIBRARY & GNOSTIC GOSPELS

### Discovery Context
- **Discovered:** December 1945, near Nag Hammadi, Egypt
- **Found by:** Muhammad Ali al-Samman (local farmer)
- **Contents:** 13 leather-bound codices, 52 texts total
- **Date of manuscripts:** ~350-400 CE (Coptic translations)
- **Original composition:** 1st-3rd centuries CE
- **Significance:** Largest collection of Gnostic Christian texts

---

## 1. GOSPEL OF THOMAS ⭐ MOST IMPORTANT

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** Highly debated
- Early dating: 50-70 CE (before canonical gospels)
- Late dating: 100-140 CE (after canonical gospels)
- Scholarly consensus: 80-140 CE

**Surviving Manuscripts:**
1. **Complete Coptic Version** (~350 CE)
   - Location: Nag Hammadi Codex II
   - Current home: Coptic Museum, Cairo, Egypt
   - Condition: Nearly complete

2. **Greek Fragments** (~200 CE) - 500+ years older!
   - **P.Oxy 1** (Oxyrhynchus Papyrus 1)
     - Contains: Sayings 26-33, 77
     - Location: Bodleian Library, Oxford
   - **P.Oxy 654**
     - Contains: Prologue + Sayings 1-7
     - Location: British Library, London
   - **P.Oxy 655**
     - Contains: Sayings 36-39
     - Location: Houghton Library, Harvard

**Language:** Greek original → Coptic translation

### Content
**Total Sayings:** 114 logia (sayings)
**Format:** Pure sayings collection, no narrative framework
**Opening:** "These are the secret sayings that the living Jesus spoke and Didymos Judas Thomas recorded."

**Overlap with Canonical Gospels:** ~35-50% of sayings have parallels

**Notable Unique Sayings:**

**Saying 2:**
> "Jesus said: Let him who seeks, not cease seeking until he finds, and when he finds, he will be troubled, and when he has been troubled, he will marvel and he will reign over the All."

**Saying 77:** (Most controversial)
> "Jesus said: I am the light that is over all. I am the All. The All came forth out of me, and the All attained unto me. Split wood—I am there. Raise up the stone, and you shall find me there."

**Saying 13:** (Jesus's identity question)
> "Jesus said to his disciples: Make a comparison and tell me whom I am like. Simon Peter said to him: You are like a righteous angel. Matthew said to him: You are like a wise philosopher. Thomas said to him: Master, my mouth is wholly incapable of saying whom you are like. Jesus said: I am not your master, because you have drunk, you have become intoxicated from the bubbling spring which I have measured out."

**Saying 114:** (Controversial gender statement)
> "Simon Peter said to them: Let Mary go out from among us, because women are not worthy of the Life. Jesus said: See, I shall lead her, so that I will make her male, that she too may become a living spirit, resembling you males. For every woman who makes herself male will enter the Kingdom of Heaven."

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** Moderate to High
- Some sayings may preserve authentic oral traditions
- Others clearly later Gnostic elaborations
- Scholars debate whether it contains pre-Gospel traditions
- Thomas community may have preserved independent Jesus tradition

**Theological Perspective:** Proto-Gnostic/Wisdom tradition
- Emphasis on secret knowledge (gnosis)
- Kingdom of God as present reality, not future
- Salvation through self-knowledge
- Less apocalyptic than canonical gospels

### Why Excluded from Canon
- Discovered too late (1945) for ancient canon debates
- Greek fragments were known but not recognized as "gospel"
- Gnostic theology incompatible with orthodox Christianity
- No narrative structure (birth, ministry, death, resurrection)
- Sayings contradict canonical teachings in places

### Access
**English Translations:**
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007) - Marvin Meyer, ed.
- *The Gospel of Thomas: The Hidden Sayings of Jesus* (HarperSanFrancisco, 1992) - Marvin Meyer
- Free online: Gnosis.org, Early Christian Writings, Sacred Texts

**Critical Editions:**
- *The Gospel according to Thomas* (Brill, 1959) - A. Guillaumont et al.
- *The Gospel of Thomas and Christian Wisdom* (Seabury, 1983) - Stephen J. Patterson

**Public Domain:** Yes, for ancient text; translations vary

## 2. GOSPEL OF PHILIP

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** 180-250 CE (scholarly consensus)
**Language:** Coptic translation of Greek original (lost)
**Surviving Manuscript:** Nag Hammadi Codex II (same codex as Thomas)
**Current Location:** Coptic Museum, Cairo
**Condition:** Incomplete, significant gaps

### Content
**Total Sayings:** 15-17 attributed to Jesus
**Format:** Collection of excerpts, meditations, and sayings (not narrative)
**Theological School:** Valentinian Gnosticism

**Overlap with Canon:** ~47% has parallels or echoes

**Notable Sayings:**

**On the Name "Christian":**
> "Those who say, 'The Lord first died and then rose,' are mistaken. For he first rose and then died. If someone does not first attain the resurrection, will that person not die?"

**On Mary Magdalene:** (Most famous passage)
> "The companion of the [Savior is] Mary Magdalene. [But Christ loved] her more than [all] the disciples, and used to kiss her [often] on her [mouth]. The rest of [the disciples were offended by it and expressed disapproval]. They said to him, 'Why do you love her more than all of us?' The Savior answered and said to them, 'Why do I not love you like her?'"

Note: Brackets indicate damaged/missing text. Word "mouth" is restoration; could be "forehead" or "cheek."

**On Bridal Chamber Sacrament:**
> "The Lord did everything in a mystery: baptism, chrism, eucharist, redemption, and bridal chamber."

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** Low
- Clearly 2nd-3rd century Gnostic theology
- Valentinian technical terms and concepts
- Symbolic/allegorical interpretations foreign to 1st century
- No independent early tradition

**Theological Perspective:** Valentinian Gnostic
- Sacramental mysticism
- Union with divine through gnosis
- Resurrection as present spiritual reality
- Complex mythology of aeons and emanations

### Why Excluded
- Late composition (post-canonical period)
- Gnostic theology rejected by proto-orthodox church
- Not discovered until 1945
- Mary Magdalene material challenged church hierarchy

### Access
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007)
- Free online: Gnosis.org, Early Christian Writings

## 3. GOSPEL OF MARY (MAGDALENE)

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** 120-180 CE
**Language:** Greek original → Coptic translation

**Surviving Manuscripts:**
1. **Papyrus Berolinensis 8502** (Berlin Codex)
   - Date: Early 5th century CE
   - Language: Coptic (Sahidic dialect)
   - Location: Egyptian Museum, Berlin (Ägyptisches Museum)
   - Condition: Missing pages 1-6, 11-14 (about half the text)
   - **NOTE:** NOT from Nag Hammadi! Purchased 1896, published 1955

2. **P.Rylands 463**
   - Date: 3rd century CE (earlier!)
   - Language: Greek
   - Location: John Rylands Library, University of Manchester
   - Condition: Small fragment

3. **P.Oxyrhynchus L 3525**
   - Date: 3rd century CE
   - Language: Greek
   - Condition: Fragment

### Content
**Total Text:** Only about 8 pages survive of estimated 18-19 pages
**Format:** Dialogue between risen Jesus and disciples, then Mary's vision

**Structure:**
- Pages 1-6: Missing (probably resurrection appearance)
- Pages 7-10: Jesus teaching to disciples, commission, departure
- Pages 11-14: Missing (beginning of Mary's vision)
- Pages 15-19: Mary recounts vision, Peter and Andrew dispute her

**Notable Passages:**

**Jesus to Disciples (before departure):**
> "Beware that no one lead you astray saying, 'Lo, here!' or 'Lo, there!' For the Son of Man is within you. Follow after him! Those who seek him will find him."

**Mary's Authority Challenged:**
> "Peter said to Mary: 'Sister, we know that the Savior loved you more than the rest of women. Tell us the words of the Savior which you remember—which you know but we do not, nor have we heard them.'"

**Peter's Objection:**
> "Peter answered and spoke concerning these same things. He questioned them about the Savior: 'Did He really speak privately with a woman and not openly to us? Are we to turn about and all listen to her? Did He prefer her to us?'"

**Levi's Defense of Mary:**
> "Levi answered and said to Peter: 'Peter, you have always been hot-tempered. Now I see you contending against the woman like the adversaries. But if the Savior made her worthy, who are you indeed to reject her? Surely the Savior knows her very well. That is why He loved her more than us.'"

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** Low for Jesus sayings, High for early church conflict
- Reflects 2nd century debates about women's authority
- Jesus sayings likely composed, not preserved oral tradition
- Valuable for understanding early Christian diversity
- Shows conflict between Petrine and Magdalene traditions

**Theological Perspective:** Gnostic (possibly Valentinian)
- Salvation through vision and knowledge
- Soul's ascent through hostile cosmic powers
- Challenges apostolic succession through Peter
- Women's spiritual authority

### Why Excluded
- Late composition (2nd century)
- Challenges male apostolic authority
- Gnostic theology
- Discovered in modern era

### Access
- *The Gospel of Mary of Magdala* (Polebridge, 2003) - Karen L. King
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (includes it despite not being from NH)
- Free online: Early Christian Writings, Gnosis.org

## 4. GOSPEL OF TRUTH

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** 140-180 CE
**Possible Author:** Valentinus himself (Gnostic teacher in Rome ~140-160 CE)
**Language:** Coptic translation of Greek original

**Surviving Manuscripts:**
1. **Nag Hammadi Codex I (Jung Codex)**
   - Condition: Nearly complete
   - Current Location: Jung Institute, Zurich → Coptic Museum, Cairo
2. **Nag Hammadi Codex XII**
   - Condition: Fragmentary

### Content
**Format:** Theological meditation/homily, NOT a sayings collection
**Genre:** More like a sermon than a gospel
**Length:** About 9,000 words

**Key Themes:**
- Error/ignorance as cause of suffering
- Jesus as manifestation of divine Truth/Word
- Salvation through knowledge of the Father
- No narrative, no sayings collection

**Notable Passages:**

**Opening:**
> "The gospel of truth is joy for those who have received from the Father of truth the gift of recognizing him, through the power of the Word that came forth from the pleroma."

**On Jesus's Mission:**
> "He came by means of fleshly form, while nothing blocked his course because incorruptibility is irresistible. Moreover, speaking new things, still speaking about what is in the heart of the Father, he brought forth the flawless word."

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** None for Jesus quotes
- No direct Jesus sayings preserved
- Theological reflection, not historical tradition
- Possibly written by Valentinus himself
- Shows developed Gnostic Christology

**Theological Perspective:** Valentinian Gnosticism
- Error (plane) as cosmic force
- Gnosis as salvation
- Father as unknowable depth
- Jesus as revealing Word

### Why Excluded
- Not a "gospel" in traditional sense (no biography, no sayings)
- Gnostic theology
- Late composition
- No historical Jesus material

### Access
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007)
- Free online: Gnosis.org

## 5. APOCRYPHON OF JAMES

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** 100-200 CE (possibly before 185 CE when Irenaeus may reference it)
**Language:** Coptic translation of Greek original
**Manuscript:** Nag Hammadi Codex I (Jung Codex)
**Condition:** Complete

### Content
**Format:** Secret revelation of Jesus to James and Peter, 550 days after resurrection
**Frame:** Letter from James to an unnamed recipient
**Genre:** Post-resurrection dialogue gospel

**Notable Teaching:**

**On Prophecy and Salvation:**
> "Become earnest about the word! For as to the word, its first part is faith; the second, love; the third, works; for from these comes life. For the word is like a grain of wheat; when someone had sown it, he had faith in it; and when it had sprouted, he loved it because he had seen many grains in place of one. And when he had worked, he was saved because he had prepared it for food, (and) again he left (some) to sow."

**Jesus on Suffering:**
> "Do you not desire therefore to be filled? And your heart is drunk; do you not desire therefore to be sober? Therefore be ashamed! And now, waking or sleeping, remember that you have seen the Son of Man, and with him you have spoken, and to him you have listened."

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** Low
- Post-resurrection dialogue is literary device
- Reflects 2nd century theological debates
- James as authority figure reflects Jewish Christianity
- No authentic historical Jesus material likely

**Theological Perspective:** Proto-Gnostic, Jewish Christian influence
- Emphasis on James (brother of Jesus) as primary apostle
- Suffering and persecution as path to salvation
- Secret revelation after resurrection
- Tension with Petrine authority

### Why Excluded
- Late composition
- Secret revelation framework suspicious to orthodox
- Proto-Gnostic elements
- Discovered 1945

### Access
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007)

## 6. APOCRYPHON OF JOHN

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** Before 180 CE (referenced by Irenaeus)
**Language:** Greek original → Coptic translation

**Surviving Manuscripts:** FOUR versions!
1. **Berlin Codex 8502** (purchased 1896)
2. **Nag Hammadi Codex II**
3. **Nag Hammadi Codex III**
4. **Nag Hammadi Codex IV**

**Versions:** Two "long" and two "short" recensions

### Content
**Format:** Revelation dialogue between risen Christ and John
**Genre:** Gnostic creation myth and cosmology

**Key Content:**
- Complex cosmology of aeons and emanations
- **Yaldabaoth** (Demiurge) as creator of material world
- Sophia's fall and restoration
- Salvation history from Adam to Christ
- Heavenly Seth as redeemer figure

**Opening Frame:**
> "It happened one day, when John, the brother of James—who are the sons of Zebedee—had come up to the temple, that a Pharisee named Arimanius approached him and said to him, 'Where is your master whom you followed?' And he said to him, 'He has gone to the place from which he came.' The Pharisee said to him, 'With deception did this Nazarene deceive you...'"

**Christ's Self-Revelation:**
> "I am the Father, I am the Mother, I am the Son. I am the undefiled and incorruptible one."

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** None
- Fully developed Gnostic mythology (2nd century)
- No historical Jesus material
- Sethian Gnostic theological system
- Creation myth incompatible with Jewish/Christian tradition

**Theological Perspective:** Sethian Gnosticism
- Material world created by evil/ignorant demiurge (Yaldabaoth)
- Humans have divine spark trapped in matter
- Seth as heavenly redeemer
- Complex emanation cosmology

### Why Excluded
- Radically dualistic (matter = evil)
- Contradicts Genesis creation
- Late Gnostic theology
- No historical basis

### Access
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007)
- Multiple scholarly editions due to four manuscripts

## 7. DIALOGUE OF THE SAVIOUR

### Manuscript Details
**Composition Date:** 
- Core material: Possibly late 1st century (80-120 CE)
- Final form: ~150 CE
**Manuscript:** Nag Hammadi Codex III
**Condition:** Fragmentary, significant gaps

### Content
**Format:** Dialogue between Jesus and three disciples: Judas (Thomas), Matthew, Mary (Magdalene)
**Genre:** Wisdom dialogue

**Significance:** May preserve VERY early tradition, possibly contemporary with Gospel of John

**Notable Passages:**

**Mary's Question:**
> "Mary said, 'Tell me, Lord, why have I come to this place to profit or to forfeit?' The Lord said, 'You make manifest the abundance of the revealer.'"

**On Knowledge:**
> "His disciples said to him, 'What is the place to which we shall go?' The Lord said, 'The place which you can reach, stand there!'"

**Cosmological Teaching:**
> "Judas said, 'Tell me, Lord, what is the beginning of the way?' He said, 'Love and goodness. For if one of these had existed among the governors, wickedness would never have come into existence.'"

### Scholarly Assessment
**Authenticity Potential:** Moderate to High for core material
- May contain independent early tradition
- Some scholars date core to 1st century (pre-John)
- Wisdom dialogue format similar to Q source
- Later Gnostic editing added to earlier core

**Theological Perspective:** Wisdom tradition → Gnostic development
- Earlier layer: Jewish wisdom sayings
- Later layer: Gnostic cosmology
- Mary Magdalene as prominent disciple
- Thomas (Judas) as questioner

### Why Excluded
- Fragmentary condition
- Gnostic final form
- Discovered 1945
- Multiple layers make it complex

### Access
- *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007)
- *The Dialogue of the Savior* (Brill, 1984) - Stephen Emmel

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

### Texts We Actually Have (Complete or Substantial)
1. **Gospel of Thomas** - Most important, 114 sayings, Greek + Coptic
2. **Gospel of Philip** - Valentinian, Mary Magdalene material
3. **Gospel of Mary** - Women's authority, 50% lost
4. **Apocryphon of James** - Post-resurrection dialogue
5. **Apocryphon of John** - Four manuscripts, Gnostic cosmology
6. **Dialogue of the Saviour** - May have earliest core

### Highest Authenticity Potential
1. **Gospel of Thomas** - Some sayings may be authentic oral tradition
2. **Dialogue of the Saviour** - Core may predate John
3. Other Nag Hammadi texts - Mostly 2nd-3rd century compositions

### Where to Access Texts
**Best Single Source:** *The Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (HarperOne, 2007)
- Edited by Marvin Meyer
- All 52 texts in one volume
- Excellent introductions

**Online (Free):**
- Gnosis.org
- EarlyChristianWritings.com
- Sacred-Texts.com

**Critical Editions:**
- Brill Academic Publishers (individual texts)
- Various scholars' specialized editions
